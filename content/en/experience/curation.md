## Curatorial Profile

I am a curator with a background in interaction design, game design, and new media art. My curatorial practice follows the changing relationships among art, design, technology, and society, with projects spanning young international artists, artificial intelligence, robotics, interactive installation, future lifestyles, and interdisciplinary design. Through exhibitions, I try to build connections among creators, technology, space, and the public. Compared with curatorial approaches centered mainly on art-historical research and object display, my method is more grounded in interaction design, user experience, and systems thinking. I care not only about what a work expresses, but also about how audiences enter an exhibition, understand the work, move through the space, and form emotional connections with abstract questions in art and technology.

For me, curation is not simply placing a group of works in the same room. It is building a narrative system made of themes, works, space, media, visitor flow, and public communication. A curator needs to identify relationships among works and information from different backgrounds, build a clear content structure, and translate complex issues into cultural experiences that the public can see, understand, participate in, and discuss.

My curatorial practice began with a focus on exchange and exhibition opportunities for young international creators.

In 2021, when pandemic restrictions limited international exchange and offline exhibitions, I co-initiated **OMNI Art Expo** with student organizations from art schools in China and abroad. The project connected student associations from 10 art and design colleges across the United States, launched an open call for interdisciplinary young creators, received more than 1,000 submissions, and selected around 200 works for a group exhibition at Xintai Warehouse in Shanghai.

**OMNI Art Expo** was not only an exhibition of young artists' work, but also a cross-school, cross-cultural, and interdisciplinary curatorial experiment. The participating works covered visual art, product design, fashion design, architecture, interaction design, new media art, and experimental practice. My work included project initiation, curatorial theme and content direction, school and creator coordination, open-call management, work selection, exhibition structure, spatial display design, visual materials, and on-site delivery.

The exhibition venue, Shanghai Xintai Warehouse, is a century-old industrial building that once served as a textile factory. With its exposed brick walls, industrial structure, and visible historical traces, we did not treat the venue as a neutral white box. We treated the building itself as part of the exhibition narrative.

In the exhibition design, we extended the building's original brick texture and used stacked bricks in the main hall to form display structures, allowing contemporary design works to enter into direct dialogue with the site's industrial heritage. By placing Parsons fashion design works and Cornell-related design drawings within the same spatial relationship, the exhibition brought together different disciplines, materials, and creative methods, creating a collision between industrial history and future-facing design language.

OMNI Art Expo was the first large cross-school art project I helped carry from concept to public exhibition. It required curatorial judgment and content organization, as well as coordination across schools, creators, venues, construction teams, communications, and public resources. The project ultimately received support and visits from the Shanghai municipal government, which made me realize that curation can connect scattered creative resources, urban cultural spaces, and public life.

After OMNI Art Expo, my curatorial research gradually expanded from young international art and interdisciplinary design toward technology art, robotics, and the future.

In 2025, I participated as a curator in planning the **2025 International Robotics and Art Design Biennale**. The project was held from August 2 to September 30, 2025, at Robot World in Beijing E-Town, and served as a companion art and design exhibition for the 2025 World Robot Conference.

The exhibition centered on the theme "Future Symbionts: Robots, Art, and Humanity," exploring how robots are moving beyond industrial production and technical efficiency into culture, emotion, home life, artistic creation, and social relationships. The project invited submissions from leading Chinese art schools, overseas art and design colleges, young artists, robot designers, and interdisciplinary research teams. It received more than 200 works and selected more than 40 for exhibition.

In this project, I contributed to curatorial theme definition, the exhibition content framework, international open calls, work selection and review, artist and school communication, exhibition-zone structure, spatial content organization, visitor flow, visual materials, venue preparation, light renovation, and on-site presentation.

With content ranging from robot products, AI art, generative design, intelligent interactive installations, bionic machinery, and virtual reality to future-home design and international design award works, I needed to establish a curatorial logic that was unified but open. The goal was to let works from art, design, technology, and industry form a dialogue within the same exhibition instead of appearing as isolated technical displays.

In selecting works, I considered not only visual impact and technical completion, but also conceptual integrity, originality, social value, on-site presentation conditions, and public communicability. For works using artificial intelligence and robotics, I paid particular attention to whether the technology genuinely shaped the work's expression or merely functioned as surface packaging.

The exhibition did not treat robots simply as technological products on display. Instead, it regarded them as new subjects entering human daily life and social relationships. Through combinations of different works, the exhibition raised a series of questions: when robots begin to have human-like bodies, language, and emotional expression, how will humans rethink intelligence, labor, companionship, and creation? When artificial intelligence begins to participate in art production, how will the relationship among creators, tools, and works change?

To make frontier technology more accessible to general audiences, I considered content research, visual experience, and public participation together within the exhibition structure. Through sections, spatial flow, and narrative rhythm, I guided audiences from an intuitive understanding of robot forms and functions toward deeper questions about human-machine relationships, emotional projection, and the futures these technologies suggest.

From OMNI Art Expo to the 2025 International Robotics and Art Design Biennale, my curatorial practice gradually formed a clear path: first connecting young international art and design communities, then exploring interdisciplinary display within historical architecture, and later organizing public conversations around robotics, artificial intelligence, and technology art.

These two curatorial experiences also changed how I understand the role of a curator.

In OMNI Art Expo, I was focused on building a platform for exhibition and exchange among young creators across schools. In the International Robotics and Art Design Biennale, I became more interested in how curation can respond to technological change, and how art, design, industry, and the public can enter dialogue around future issues.

My interaction design background helps me understand audience behavior and information reception in space. My game design experience makes me pay attention to participation rules, experience rhythm, and feedback mechanisms. My new media and technical practice also helps me understand the implementation logic behind artworks and communicate effectively with artists, designers, engineers, and technical teams.

Together, these experiences formed my curatorial method:

**Build a point of view through research, organize content through narrative, shape experience through design, and bring exhibitions into reality through interdisciplinary collaboration.**

I believe curation is not only the selection and display of existing works, but also an active form of knowledge production. Curators need to discover connections among different disciplines, cultures, and creative methods, raise questions with public value through exhibitions, and create spaces where different viewpoints can appear together.

I hope to use curation to connect art and technology, history and future, creators and the public, academic research and industry practice. In technology art exhibitions, I care not only about "what technology can do," but also about the questions that follow: "Why do we need it?", "How is it changing us?", and "What kind of future do we want to move toward with technology?"

My goal is not to add artistic decoration to technology, nor to turn art into an explanation of technical achievement. It is to let art, design, and technology challenge one another, and to let them participate together in imagining and shaping the future.
