## USC: From Interaction Design to Games and Immersive Experiences


In 2022, I entered the Interactive Media & Games program at the USC School of Cinematic Arts and began systematically exploring interaction design, game design, and immersive experiences. There, I gradually expanded my interface-centered design methods into more complex spatial interaction, game mechanics, and emerging media. I also began thinking about a larger question: when interaction moves from two-dimensional screens into three-dimensional space, how should designers redefine the relationship between people and the digital world?

## NeonCity VR: Redesigning "City Building" in Virtual Space


During my time at USC, I participated in the advanced game design project NeonCity VR and served as Lead Interaction Designer, mainly responsible for VR interfaces, spatial interaction, and hand gesture operation experience.

NeonCity VR is an immersive VR city-building game centered on future city planning and free creation. Players can use natural hand gestures to grab, move, and combine different building modules, gradually creating their own city in virtual space.

Unlike traditional city simulation games, NeonCity truly brings city building into three-dimensional space. Players can plan and allocate resources from the perspective of a miniature city, or switch at any time to a 1:1 human scale, entering the neighborhoods they built by hand, walking and exploring inside the city, and sensing how their design decisions affect the entire city through feedback from residents and the environment.

In the project, I focused on gesture interaction, Spatial UI, the city-building flow, and the transition between different spatial scales. I hoped to transform originally complex city simulation operations into more natural and intuitive VR interactions, giving players the feeling of "creating a city with both hands."

The project was developed over nearly two years. During that period, we also invited designers from Rockstar Games to participate in playtests and provide feedback on the core experience from a professional game design perspective. Eventually, the NeonCity VR Demo was officially released on [Meta](https://www.meta.com/experiences/pcvr/neon-city-demo/6015050278577275/?srsltid=AfmBOoohSGrEIytVdpGTD8rgYw-4dUKZhoGb900_NcZYIUqkigko35nr).

## ARtistic Tales: Making AR Part of Children's Reading


Beyond VR game design, I also participated in an industry-academic AR research course jointly offered by TikTok Effect House and Snap AR, and led the design and development of ARtistic Tales.

ARtistic Tales is an AR interactive reading product for children aged 6-10, with particular attention to children with dyslexia. Through augmented reality and multisensory interaction, it aims to lower the barriers to comprehension and participation in traditional reading.

The project combines physical picture books with AR scenes, allowing characters and story environments from the book to "enter reality" as 3D animations. Children can read while seeing characters appear in the book and surrounding space, and can choose reading modes that better match their ability through accessible features such as synchronized text and voice reading, font size and reading speed adjustment, and a full audio mode.

Beyond reading, we further added interactive mini-games such as reading comprehension, spelling, and drag-and-match activities. Combined with badges, continuous reading records, and content unlock mechanisms, the project built a complete learning experience:

**Immersive reading -> interactive practice -> instant feedback -> growth incentives**

What I wanted to explore through this project was not only "how to make reading more interesting with AR," but how to use a new interaction medium to help children with different reading abilities enter stories more naturally, understand content, and gradually build participation and confidence in reading.

ARtistic Tales later won a MUSE Design Award. The related research paper was accepted by ICHEC 2025 and included in the ACM Digital Library. [Read the ARtistic Tales paper and full project introduction](/en/paper/artistic-tales).

## World in a Cell

In early 2023, after multiple rounds of selection, I was fortunate to join USC World Building Media Lab's interdisciplinary research project World in a Cell as a staff member, mainly serving as VR Interaction Designer & Researcher. Through VR, spatial storytelling, and world building, the project transformed complex cell structures and biological information into an immersive virtual world that users could enter, observe, and explore.

In the project, I participated in VR interaction experience research and design, exploring how users could move, observe, select, and access information more naturally in three-dimensional space. I also participated in early-stage research, translating complex scientific content into interaction logic and experience paths better suited for immersive media. I paid particular attention to balancing scientific accuracy with users' comprehension cost, so that professional knowledge was not merely "visualized," but could truly be perceived and understood through space, behavior, and feedback.

This was also my first deep involvement in an interdisciplinary Science x VR x World Building project. It made me realize that interaction design can not only solve the problem of "how an interface works," but can also become a translation medium connecting scientific knowledge with public understanding. This experience further influenced my later explorations in VR, AR, educational experiences, and immersive interaction.
