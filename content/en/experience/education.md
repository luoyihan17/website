## USC: From Interaction Design to Games and Immersive Experiences


In 2022, I entered the Interactive Media & Games program at the USC School of Cinematic Arts, where I explored interaction design, game design, and immersive experiences. I expanded my interface-centered practice into spatial interaction, game systems, and emerging media, and began asking how design changes when interaction moves from screens into 3D space.

## NeonCity VR: Redesigning "City Building" in Virtual Space


At USC, I joined the advanced game project NeonCity VR as Lead Interaction Designer, focusing on VR interfaces, spatial interaction, and hand gestures.

NeonCity VR is an immersive city-building game about future planning and free creation. Players use natural hand gestures to grab, move, and combine building modules, gradually creating their own city.

Unlike traditional city simulations, NeonCity brings city building into 3D space. Players can plan from a miniature view, then switch to 1:1 scale and walk through the neighborhoods they built, using resident and environmental feedback to understand their decisions.

I focused on gesture interaction, Spatial UI, city-building flow, and transitions between spatial scales, turning complex simulation controls into a more natural VR experience: creating a city with both hands.

The project ran for nearly two years. We also invited Rockstar Games designers to join playtests and give professional feedback. The NeonCity VR Demo was later released on [Meta](https://www.meta.com/experiences/pcvr/neon-city-demo/6015050278577275/?srsltid=AfmBOoohSGrEIytVdpGTD8rgYw-4dUKZhoGb900_NcZYIUqkigko35nr).

## ARtistic Tales: Making AR Part of Children's Reading


Beyond VR game design, I joined an AR research course by TikTok Effect House and Snap AR, and led the design and development of ARtistic Tales.

ARtistic Tales is an AR reading product for children aged 6-10, with special attention to children with dyslexia. Through AR and multisensory interaction, it lowers the barrier to reading comprehension and participation.

The project combines physical picture books with AR scenes, letting characters and story environments "enter reality" as 3D animations. Children can read while seeing the story around them, with accessible options such as synced text and voice, font size and reading speed controls, and a full audio mode.

Beyond reading, we added mini-games for comprehension, spelling, and drag-and-match tasks. With badges, streaks, and unlocks, the project built a learning loop:

**Immersive reading -> interactive practice -> instant feedback -> growth incentives**

I wanted to explore more than how AR can make reading fun. I wanted to use a new medium to help children with different reading abilities enter stories, understand content, and build confidence.

ARtistic Tales later won a MUSE Design Award. The related research paper was accepted by ICHEC 2025 and included in the ACM Digital Library. [Read the ARtistic Tales paper and full project introduction](/en/paper/artistic-tales).

## World in a Cell

In early 2023, I joined USC World Building Media Lab's interdisciplinary research project World in a Cell as VR Interaction Designer & Researcher. Through VR, spatial storytelling, and world building, the project turned complex cell structures and biological information into a virtual world users could enter, observe, and explore.

I worked on VR interaction research and design, exploring how users could move, observe, select, and access information in 3D space. I also helped translate scientific content into interaction logic for immersive media, balancing scientific accuracy with comprehension cost.

This was my first deep Science x VR x World Building project. It showed me that interaction design can do more than explain how an interface works; it can translate scientific knowledge into public understanding. That experience shaped my later work in VR, AR, education, and immersive interaction.
