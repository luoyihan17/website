## Project Background

AI Creation is one of the core AI music creation projects I designed for QQ Music. It refers to a creation flow in which users can generate a complete song with AI through a single sentence, a conversation, an image, humming, or style selection.

As AIGC enters music scenarios, QQ Music is no longer only a platform for listening to, collecting, and sharing music. It is also becoming an entry point where everyday users can express emotions, record life moments, generate works, and participate in music creation. The goal of AI song creation is not to serve a professional music production workflow, but to help users without lyric-writing, composing, or arranging experience create a song of their own with a low barrier.

## Product Experience Thinking in AI Music Creation

When I received AI song creation requirements, I did not simply understand the feature as a tool where users "input a prompt -> generate a song." Instead, I first compared products such as Suno and thought about how QQ Music, as a mass-market music platform, differs in user mindset, usage scenarios, and willingness to pay.

Suno is closer to a professional or semi-professional creator tool. Users often enter with clear creative goals, such as generating a complete song, trying a specific music style, or creating continuously. Most QQ Music users, however, are not professional creators. Their first reaction to AI song creation is more about curiosity, playfulness, emotional expression, and sharing with people around them.

So when designing the AI song creation experience, my focus was not to make the feature more "professional," but to ask:

> How can we help users start creating with low effort, and create reasons for payment and sharing before and after the generated result?

### 1. From Tool-based Creation to Scenario-based Creation

I observed that when everyday users use AI song creation, they are often not trying to become musicians. They want to quickly generate a song that can be given to someone, express an emotion, or join a topic.

Typical scenarios include:

- Generating birthday songs or blessing songs for friends
- Generating anniversary, confession, or apology songs for romantic partners
- Artist fans generating support songs for idols or singers they like
- Users generating personalized songs from their emotions, photos, or a single sentence
- Quickly generating shareable content for holidays, trending topics, or campaigns

So in design, I prioritized creating clear scenarios for users instead of offering only a blank input box. A blank input box has a high cognitive cost for ordinary users: they do not know what to write, or what kind of input will produce a good song.

A better approach for QQ Music is:

> Give users a reason for why they want to create a song first, then lower the barrier for how to create it.

For example, relationship scenarios, holiday scenarios, emotional scenarios, fan scenarios, and campaign topics can help users enter a creative state more quickly.

In this part, my priority was to help users find a clear reason to create. Relationship, holiday, and fan support scenarios can trigger motivation faster. Only when "why create a song" is established do the following input methods, style choices, and generation experiences gain a clear direction.

### 2. Creation Encouragement Mechanisms

To increase users' willingness to keep creating, the product manager and I jointly conceived mechanisms such as work release, play data, ranking lists, and certificates of honor. Play counts and rankings mainly strengthen users' sense of competition, allowing works to move beyond being saved locally after generation and enter a public creative field where they can be compared and seen. Certificates and work displays strengthen users' sense of achievement, helping them perceive their AI song as an officially released work rather than a temporary generated result.

![QQ Music AI Music Ranking List and Rewards](/assets/images/AIMusic/Ranking%20List%20%26%20Rewards.webp)

### 3. Business Conversion and Commercial Incentives

For commercialization, I focused on guiding users from basic generation toward higher-value features and services, such as high-quality generation, regeneration, style optimization, work release, exposure boosts, and incentive earnings. Unlike creation encouragement mechanisms, which mainly answer "why continue creating," commercial incentives focus more on "why users are willing to invest more." Among them, the incentive earnings mechanism helps users realize that AI songs can not only be heard, but may also generate revenue feedback, supporting future paid conversion, creator retention, and content ecosystem building.

![QQ Music AI Music Business Conversion](/assets/images/AIMusic/Business%20Conversion.webp)

### 4. Entertainment-oriented Experience Design

To increase users' motivation to keep exploring, I participated in designing experiences such as AIMV, image analysis, and AI adaptation, extending AI song creation from a single generation tool into a more process-rich, visual, and interactive entertainment experience. AIMV strengthens the viewing and sharing value of generated results, allowing a song to further become a shareable music video. Image analysis lowers the cost of inspiration input, helping users quickly find creative direction from photos, scenes, and emotions. AI adaptation lets users continue trying different styles, emotions, and forms of expression based on existing results, increasing the motivation for repeated creation. Through these designs, users do not merely complete one song generation. They become willing to keep trying different materials, styles, and outcomes, transforming the experience from "daring to start" to "wanting to keep playing."

## Project Summary

Through the AI song creation project, my biggest realization is that AI product design is not simply about showing technical capability. It is about finding the reasons users are truly willing to use and keep participating.

For QQ Music users, AI song creation is more like a low-barrier form of expression and entertainment. In my design, I focused on scenario-based entry points, waiting feedback, entertainment-oriented play, and creation incentives, helping users gradually move from "trying it out" to "wanting to keep creating."

This project also helped me more clearly understand that product experience designers in the AI era need to understand user mindset, business goals, and technical boundaries at the same time, translating complex capabilities into simple, interesting, and sustainable user experiences.
